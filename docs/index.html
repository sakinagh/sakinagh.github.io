About Me

I am a Ph.D. student in Experimental Psychology at Texas Christian University (TCU) and a Graduate Research Assistant at the Institute of Behavioral Research (IBR). My academic work operates at the intersection of behavioral psychology, data science, and technology.

I study how advanced tools can be leveraged to build scalable interventions that optimize future decision-making and promote positive behavior change across public health and organizational settings.

Background

Prior to my doctoral studies, I earned a Bachelor of Science in Psychology with a minor in Data Science from TCU. This cross-disciplinary background allows me to bridge complex psychological theory with technical application. Additionally, I have been involved at the IBR since 2022, and my professional background includes analyzing technology development and user experience at JPMorgan Chase, as well as researching AI's role in educational outcomes at a startup.

Research Interests

My primary research areas focus on the design and evaluation of technology-driven behavioral solutions. My research leverages technology and data science to understand and facilitate behavior change, with applications in public health and organizational settings. My work is driven by an interest in exploring scalable interventions that are responsive to individual health challenges and future decision-making.
